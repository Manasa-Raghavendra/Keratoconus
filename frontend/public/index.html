About Keratoconus

Understanding the condition and AI-assisted diagnosis

What is Keratoconus?

Keratoconus is a progressive eye disease where the normally round cornea thins and begins to bulge into a cone-like shape. This cone shape deflects light as it enters the eye on its way to the light-sensitive retina, causing distorted vision.

  • Affects approximately 1 in 2,000 people
  • Usually begins during teenage years
  • Progressive condition requiring monitoring
  • Can affect one or both eyes

Symptoms

Early detection is crucial for effective management. Common symptoms include:

  • Blurred or distorted vision
  • Increased sensitivity to light and glare
  • Frequent changes in eyeglass prescription
  • Difficulty driving at night
  • Eye strain and headaches
  • Halos around lights

Pentacam Examination

Pentacam is a rotating Scheimpflug camera system that provides comprehensive anterior segment analysis:

  • 3D chamber analysis from anterior to posterior
  • True elevation maps (front & back)
  • Pachymetry mapping (corneal thickness)
  • Pupil diameter and position
  • Anterior chamber depth analysis
  • Keratoconus screening indices

Treatment Overview

Treatment options vary based on the stage and progression of keratoconus:

Early Stage

Glasses or soft contact lenses can correct mild astigmatism

Moderate Stage

Rigid gas permeable (RGP) lenses or scleral lenses provide better vision

Progressive Cases

Corneal cross-linking (CXL) can halt progression

Advanced Stage

Corneal transplant may be necessary in severe cases
